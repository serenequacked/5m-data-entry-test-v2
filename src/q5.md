# Question 5 — Operating Systems, Terminals, and Shells

**Format:** Short Answer

---

> Answer in your own words, 1-3 sentences each. Writing it yourself is the point.


**(a)** What is a **kernel**, in plain language? (One or two sentences. No textbook definition copied in; explain it as you understand it.)

**Answer:**
A kernel manages the computer’s hardware and resources, such as memory, processing time, and connected devices. It acts as a bridge between applications and the hardware.

---

**(b)** A classmate says:

> "I installed WSL, so now I have two completely separate operating systems, Windows and Linux, running side by side, each totally independent."

Is this accurate? Briefly explain why or why not.

**Answer:**
No that is not true, when WSL is installed on a mac device for example, it is not completely isolated as it can still access files on the mac device itseld. 

---

**(c)** Explain the difference between a **terminal (emulator)** and a **shell**, and give one example of each.

**Answer:**
A terminal emulator is the application window where you enter and view commands, while a shell is the program that reads and executes those commands. Windows Terminal is a terminal emulator, and Bash is a shell.
